River onboarding

Redesigning River’s sign up for individual and business accounts

Overview

Influencing Leadership, Experience, and Product through Design

River is a bitcoin financial institution, and every new client has to pass identity and regulatory checks before they can use their account. Sign up is the first real conversation River has with them.

As lead designer, I redesigned onboarding end to end, working with engineering through a full React rebuild and with brand and marketing while the new identity took shape.

Problem

Less steps that felt heavier than they looked

The old sign up squeezed every question into just a few steps. It looked short on paper, but each step asked for four or five inputs at once. That overload landed right when clients were deciding whether to trust us, and many dropped off before creating an account.

The copy didn’t help. It read robotic and transactional, which put distance between River and the client. For a product that has to feel trustworthy, onboarding felt cold.

Design

Asking for the right thing at the right moment

I broke the flow into a conversational sequence that asks for each piece of information when it makes sense, not all at once. The journey moves through three phases: creating the account, verifying login details, and collecting personal information. Each step asks for less, so progress feels steady instead of overwhelming.

I also rebuilt the inputs. Fields validate as clients type, addresses come from search instead of manual entry, and area code and state use dropdowns instead of free text. Small changes, but together they make each step faster to clear, so reaching the end of onboarding never feels like work.

Challenges

Rebuilding the stack while the brand changed underneath

Two things made this hard. Engineering rebuilt sign up from the ground up while River was mid-rebrand, so every screen had to keep pace with a brand still taking shape. And onboarding is full of edge cases, from identities that can’t be verified to addresses that don’t qualify. I designed each path so the experience stayed clear and trustworthy wherever a client landed.

Next step

Native, and verification as a fast lane

I’m shaping what makes native feel delightful and snappy: the logged-out experience, animations, custom illustrations for identity verification, and upsells for notifications and Face ID.

We’re also exploring moving identity verification earlier. If a client verifies up front, they could skip the personal information steps and go straight to the home page, turning verification from a hurdle into a fast lane.

Learnings & Reflection

Sign up has to keep evolving as technology and security requirements change. At that pace, the quiet risk isn’t only technical debt. It’s teams rebuilding pieces that were never designed to scale, and every rebuild makes a modern experience harder to get right.

For me, that’s the moment to step back, observe, and look at the bigger picture. Design can be the bridge: artifacts that show engineering and leadership what good looks like, and earn enough trust to justify the bigger lift a rebuild takes.

Salesforce+ Streaming Experience

A card system that increases scannability and engagement

Role

UX Strategy

Scope

Visual Design

Design System

Industry

Streaming Services

Overview

Establishing a visual communication system for Salesforce+

Salesforce is an established brand, but Salesforce+ was a new streaming platform with no design foundation at all. As its library grew, it needed a way to show everything it had to offer, so viewers could scan a row, spot what fits their interests and decide whether a title was worth their time.

Partnering with the team at Instrument, I shaped the UX strategy for a card system built around two user needs. The first was scannability: every card should convey its most important information at a glance. The second was content resonance: viewers should be able to judge whether a title fits their interests, the time they have and the format they prefer. Meeting both would drive more clicks to Play, Learn More and Save, and more sign-ups through the platform.

The system became the platform’s first visual and interaction foundation, with tags and badges that adapt as new kinds of content arrive, and accessibility and readability built in from the start.

Just Work Out Offline Mode

An offline workout tracking system

Role

UX Designer

Scope

Feature

Industry

Fitness

Problem

Losing connection meant losing the workout

Just Work Out lets Peloton members start a workout and track their metrics without picking a class. But without an internet connection, they couldn’t start a workout at all, or risked losing one already in progress. Offline support was one of the most requested features.

Investigation

Partnering with engineers to map every checkpoint

Keeping the experience consistent across edge cases meant finding every checkpoint that could affect the flow. I worked closely with engineers to find the simplest way to cover every scenario.

The device caches workout data as soon as a workout starts. When the workout ends, it checks for a connection. If there is one, it uploads the workout and syncs it to the member’s account. If not, it keeps the data cached until it can.

Outcome

Never lose a workout again

The challenge wasn’t visual. It was mapping every edge case and working with engineers to find a solution that kept the experience clear and consistent in any connection state.

The goal was to communicate workout status accurately while staying within the existing design system, keeping the solution lightweight enough to ship quickly.

NASA ICESat-2 SlideRule

Making NASA satellite data easy to explore, for the public and for scientists

Role

UX Lead

Scope

Overview

Making satellite data usable for everyone

SlideRule is NASA’s tool for requesting ICESat-2 elevation data without downloading massive files. It was built by and for scientists, which made it hard for anyone else to use.

Through SCADpro, I led UX for a partnership with NASA Goddard Space Flight Center. We studied how scientists use the tool today, then designed two paths: an approachable entry point for the public and an advanced mode that speeds up research workflows.

What ICESat-2 does

Measuring Earth’s surface from orbit

It’s a NASA satellite that fires 10,000 laser pulses per second at Earth’s surface to measure elevation precisely, from ice sheets and sea ice to forests, land and oceans.

Scientists use it to track how the planet is changing, from ice loss to shifts in vegetation and terrain.

ICESat-2 SlideRule

Asking satellite data questions without the heavy lifting

SlideRule lets anyone request ICESat-2 data without downloading massive files or processing them by hand. Pick an area of the Earth, and it pulls the relevant data from NASA’s archives and returns just what you need.

Context

Opening the data to the public, and speeding up research

We started with a deep dive into how scientists use the tool today. From there, the project had two goals: lower the barrier for the public to explore valuable satellite data, and design an advanced mode that speeds up research workflows.

Built for how researchers actually work

Researchers often run many requests and compare the results side by side. Advanced mode lets them queue requests, track each one’s status and review the results in one place.

Outcome

Validated, and still on its way

What started as a proposal became a working partnership with the ICESat-2 team. I presented the design at Goddard Space Flight Center, and the scientists who rely on SlideRule validated the direction.

SlideRule’s code is maintained by a single NASA scientist, so once the ideas were validated, we delivered a design system to make it easier to build. Changes at NASA go through several layers of review, so the redesign is still on its way. In the meantime, the proposed redesign is featured in the NASA ICESat-2 mission documentation.

River.com

A rebranded River.com that grew business and private client inquiries

Overview

One system for a rebranded River.com

River is a bitcoin financial institution for individuals, businesses and private clients. As the company rebranded, River.com needed to say more with less: concise messaging on every page that still felt premium and trustworthy.

As lead designer, I redesigned the site with our brand designer and the marketing team, and built the card and section system behind every page with another product designer.

Problem

Mismatched parts and repeated content

The old River.com mixed mismatched components with an outdated visual language. It was hard to maintain and made a weak first impression on prospective clients.

Every page repeated the same content, so visitors were overloaded and River’s value for each audience got buried.

Design

Redesigning the River.com

With our brand designer, I defined a card and section system every page could adopt while carrying the new information architecture. I also worked with the marketing team to refine the content strategy.

On the homepage, I pushed for a full-width section that marketing can rotate between campaigns, and helped produce its visual assets with the brand designer.

Building it

Implementation Informed by Design

We launched with 21 pages, and I built them with another product designer.

We started with the section components and card system, built on the new rebrand tokens, with every page behind a feature flag.

Getting the foundation right took a few days, with a senior engineer reviewing the code. After that, the two of us built pages like Lego, snapping sections together instead of starting from scratch.

Learnings & Reflection

Within three months of launch, engagement on River.com went up, and so did inquiries from business and private clients.

More than the numbers, I’m proud of how we got there. Design, engineering and marketing worked as one team, and the site now conveys River’s message more precisely.
